Buying checklist

How to choose a basic home toolkit

A useful toolkit starts with the repairs and assembly tasks you actually do. Large sets can look like a bargain, but often include duplicates or specialized tools that will go unused.

Start with essentials

Consider a measuring tape, a selection of screwdrivers or quality bits, a claw hammer, a level, adjustable wrench, pliers and a sturdy utility knife. Add a flashlight and appropriate eye protection for relevant jobs.

Evaluate how the tools feel

Look for handles you can grip comfortably, readable markings and simple storage. Where possible, evaluate fit and finish in person rather than relying on decorative packaging.

Choose storage you will use

A compact bag or box keeps common tools accessible. Leave room for future additions instead of buying more tools simply to fill a case.

Buy specialized tools when a job warrants them

If a repair involves mains electricity, gas, load-bearing structures or similar hazards, consult appropriate professionals and manufacturer instructions. A starter kit is not a substitute for training.

A simple checklist

  • List three household tasks you expect to do.
  • Choose tools suited to those tasks.
  • Favor reliable basics over huge piece counts.
  • Check warranties and replacement parts.
