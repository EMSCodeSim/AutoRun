Internet & Wi-Fi · Troubleshooting

Why is my Wi-Fi slow?

Start with the simplest distinction: is the entire internet connection slow, or is the wireless link to one device struggling?

1. Check whether one device or everything is affected

Try two devices using the same home network. If one phone is slow but a laptop works normally in the same location, investigate the phone's network connection before changing the entire router.

2. Move closer to the router

If performance improves close to the router, physical distance or interference may be contributing. Walls, placement and nearby wireless devices can all affect signal performance. You can often improve coverage by positioning networking equipment away from obstructions, following its manufacturer's recommendations.

3. Consider the internet service itself

If every device has trouble, check your internet provider's outage notices and equipment status. Wi-Fi strength bars do not necessarily indicate a working internet connection.

4. Avoid changing everything at once

Reboot an affected device if practical. If your router documentation recommends a restart, follow those steps. A factory reset can erase your Wi-Fi network name, passwords and other configuration—do not use one as an initial troubleshooting step.

Use a repeatable two-location diagnosis

Choose one device and compare the same ordinary task close to the router with that task in the room where you usually experience trouble. Repeat at similar times, avoiding simultaneous large downloads or software updates. This does not isolate every possible cause, but an improvement near the router is a useful clue that wireless coverage or interference could be involved.

If two devices are affected only in one distant room, investigate router placement and physical obstacles before buying extra network hardware. If both devices are similarly slow near the router, check your internet provider's status and any device-specific error messages before changing Wi-Fi settings.

Write down the room, device, approximate time and symptoms. Read the router positioning guide for background, but follow the router manufacturer's instructions for any changes. Do not claim a diagnosis from one speed test alone.

5. Know when to escalate

If the provider reports an outage or your connection continues to fail across devices, document what you tested and contact the provider. If only one device is affected, check that device maker's troubleshooting instructions.
