Electricity · Explainer

Does leaving a device plugged in cost money?

Some electronics use electricity in standby mode. The cost depends on the actual power draw, how long it lasts and what you pay per kilowatt-hour.

What is standby power?

Some plugged-in devices use a little electricity to keep clocks, sensors or network features running when they are not actively in use. Others draw little or no standby power. Because products differ, avoid assuming a universal wattage.

How to estimate the expense

Multiply measured or documented standby watts by hours, divide by 1,000 to get kilowatt-hours, then multiply by your rate. A hypothetical continuous 2-watt draw for 30 days uses 1.44 kWh. At $0.15 per kWh, that would cost about $0.22 for the month. This is an example, not a claim about any specific device.

Measure before deciding what to unplug

If you care about precision, a suitable consumer plug-in energy meter can help establish the real draw of compatible plug-connected devices. Follow the meter's and device's safety instructions, and do not exceed electrical ratings.

Consider the tradeoffs

Unplugging may disable network availability, schedules or automated updates. Do not unplug safety-critical equipment or a device that needs continuous operation. Estimate the savings before changing your routine.
